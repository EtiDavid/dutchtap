## Verification approach

1. **Article + English meaning + CEFR level**: cross-referenced against
   [Lenguia's frequency-ranked, CEFR-tagged Dutch word list](https://www.lenguia.com/tools/most-common-words/dutch)
   for every word sourced from that list, plus the exact example words
   given in the build spec itself (`de tas`, `het kantoor`, etc.).
2. **Plurals**: Dutch plural spelling follows real but non-trivial rules
   (consonant doubling after a short vowel: `kat→katten`; vowel-length
   reduction in an open syllable: `jaar→jaren`; f/s→v/z voicing:
   `huis→huizen`, `prijs→prijzen`; and a small set of lexical
   irregulars: `stad→steden`, `kind→kinderen`, `ei→eieren`,
   `leraar→leraren`, `dag→dagen`/`weg→wegen` as no-doubling exceptions).
   Every plural in `data/nouns.ts` was checked against this rule set by
   hand rather than generated by a `base + "en"` heuristic, specifically
   because that heuristic is exactly what produces confidently-wrong
   output (a `dak→daken` vs. `dak→dakken` mix-up was caught and
   corrected during this process). Words where the correct plural
   genuinely didn't fit these patterns with high confidence were
   excluded rather than guessed (see below).
3. **Adjectives**: `eForm` is stored explicitly per entry rather than
   derived, for the same reason — `rood→rode`, `groot→grote`,
   `goedkoop→goedkope` are all spelling changes a naive rule gets wrong.
4. Both `data/nouns.ts` and `data/adjectives.ts` are checked by
   `lib/validation/content.ts` (unique ids, required fields, valid
   article/category enums) and asserted against in `tests/content.test.ts`,
   so a future bad edit fails the test suite rather than shipping.

## Removed / uncertain entries

- **`het weer` (weather) and `het nieuws` (news)** are included in the
  bank but tagged `uncountable` with `plural` set equal to the
  singular — these genuinely have no natural plural in everyday Dutch,
  and inventing one to satisfy the schema would ship a wrong answer.
  The question generator (`lib/game/generateQuestion.ts`) explicitly
  excludes `uncountable`-tagged nouns from plural-mode questions, so
  this placeholder value is never shown to a learner. The same
  convention covers `melk`, `water`, `vlees`, `soep`, `thee`, `fruit`,
  `muziek`, `gezondheid`, `bloed`, `huiswerk`, `gras`, `sneeuw`, and
  `geld` (14 more) — mass or abstract nouns where a "correct" plural is
  either nonstandard or would misrepresent the word.
- **`het dak`** (roof) was deliberately left out of the bank. Initial
  reasoning produced `daken`; rechecking against the regular
  short-vowel-doubling rule (the same rule that gives `zak→zakken`)
  contradicted that, and rather than ship whichever guess "felt more
  familiar," the noun was dropped.
- A handful of words from the source frequency list were excluded as
  unsuitable for a beginner grammar drill rather than for uncertainty:
  slang (`gozer`, `kerel`), vulgar terms, and crime/violence vocabulary
  (`pistool`, `moord`, `gevangenis`) that doesn't fit a calm A1/A2
  learning product, per the build spec's design direction.

## Known limitation

258 nouns / 53 adjectives is comfortably above the stated minimum (250)
but below the "preferred" 300-500 range. Expanding the bank is
straightforward (append to `data/nouns.ts`/`data/adjectives.ts` and
`npm test` will validate the new entries) and is the most impactful
low-risk backlog item.
